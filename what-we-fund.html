What We Fund

Tuition is the easy part.

Most scholarships write a check to a school and call it done. That check doesn't get you to the job site at 6 a.m., and it doesn't put steel-toed boots on your feet on day one. JCD funds the whole distance — because the gap between “accepted” and “actually showed up” is where people fall out.

We’ll help you get there.
01 · The scope

Two kinds of cost.
We cover both.

Here's the reason this foundation looks different on paper: the barrier that stops a kid from starting a career usually isn't the tuition bill. It's the four hundred dollars of stuff nobody budgets for.

Bucket 01 — what everyone funds

Tuition & program fees

The certificate, the associate degree, the apprenticeship classroom hours, the licensing exam. Paid at whatever share the program and the student's situation call for.

This is table stakes. Every scholarship in the region does this.

Bucket 02 — what almost nobody funds

The costs that actually stop people

  • Getting there.A car you don't have, gas you can't cover, a bus that doesn't run to Sugar Grove. There are kids in Aurora who can't attend Waubonsee for no reason other than that they can't get there.
  • Tools.Trades apprentices supply their own. So do a lot of manufacturing hires. Nobody hands you a starter set.
  • Work boots & required clothing.Steel-toed on day one, non-negotiable, and roughly $200 before you've earned a dollar.
  • Supplies & safety gear.Books, a welding helmet, PPE. Manufacturing floors have safety requirements a new hire has to meet before they're allowed to work.
$200

“If a kid goes into the building trades, all my guys are telling me — the kids have got to get to the job. Some kids don't have an ability to get to the job. They don't have a car. Day one, boots cost 200 bucks. Kid doesn't have 200 bucks.”

Source: Bob Vaughan, June 26 2026. Verbatim.

03 · How big is an award

It depends. On purpose.

There's no flat number, and that's a deliberate design choice. An award is sized to what the program costs and what the student's situation actually requires — we don't want to be backed into a box.

Example — four-year nursing route
About half
the tuition.

A student headed into a nursing program at a private university running roughly $30K a year. JCD would expect to cover something on the order of half.

Example — community-college certificate
Up to
all of it.

A health-sciences technician certificate at around $3K. For a student with real need, that's a case where JCD covers the whole thing.

Unless the circumstances dictate 100% funding, it's not going to be a couple thousand bucks a year. It's gonna be some real money. Source: Bob Vaughan, June 26 2026. Verbatim.

04 · How the money moves

Reimbursement, not
a check to a bursar.

JCD is a 501(c)(3) private operating foundation. That structure is the reason we can reimburse real costs directly, rather than only routing tuition through an institution.

Because we're an operating foundation, we can reimburse the people for those costs. Source: Bob Vaughan, June 26 2026. Verbatim.

The practical logic is simple. Colleges don't want to administer a boots-and-bus-fare line item, and neither does a small electrical contractor — ask one to cut a separate check and you'll get a straight answer about how much they don't want to mess with it. So the most likely design is reimbursing the student directly for documented costs.

Open item — do not publish this section as settled

Bob has not finalized who the payee is. His exact position on June 26 was that he's leaning toward reimbursing the individual, followed immediately by “I said that, but I've got to research it.” This reverses the earlier, widely-repeated JCD line — “disbursed to schools and employers, never to individuals” — which still appears on printed round-1 stock handed to employers.

Confirm before launch: private foundations that make grants directly to individuals for study or training generally need IRS advance approval of their grant-selection procedures (IRC §4945(g)). Worth one call to JCD's counsel or accountant to establish whether that was part of the April 1 filing or is a separate step — it determines whether the January window can pay students directly.

05 · If you're an employer

This is a hiring
advantage.

The pitch to employers isn't charity, and we want to be explicit that it isn't. If JCD covers what falls outside what you'd normally provide, you can afford to take a chance on someone you'd otherwise pass on.

You pick who you want

JCD attaches no strings to the hire. There's no requirement to take a particular student from a particular ZIP code. That's your call, entirely.

We cover what's outside your norm

Transportation, tools, boots, required clothing — whatever sits beyond what you'd normally supply an apprentice. Your wage stays your wage.

It widens your candidate pool

An employer with this behind them has an enhancement to draw some good people. Kids who couldn't have said yes before now can.

We set expectations first

Show up, show up on time, show up prepared, and do your own research before you ask. That's the deal we make with students before they reach you.

They don't realize that if these kids have some help, they could turn out to be some really good people. But they need a little bit of help. Source: Bob Vaughan, June 26 2026. Verbatim.
06 · What we ask back

We'll help you get there.
You've got to show up.

There's no GPA requirement and no income test, so it's worth being precise about what JCD does expect. It isn't academic. It's the stuff every employer in every one of these interviews named first.

Show up

On time. One example we heard: driving the route the day before, so a train can't be the reason you're late.

Show up prepared

With your tools, your boots, your gear. That's precisely what JCD is funding, so there's no excuse left.

Do the work of learning

“Work really hard at understanding, and not asking questions that you should already research.”

Actually try it

Deciding a path isn't for you is a fine outcome — as long as you pursued it first.
