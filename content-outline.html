Why it's shaped this way

Six principles behind the outline.

Every page below earns its place against these. They're the standard we'll hold the content to when we source and write it.

01

Lead with the answer

AI answer engines (Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity) cite content that states the answer plainly, up top. Every key page opens with a direct, quotable definition or step — not a slow scroll-down.

02

One page per intent

Separate landing pages for each audience and program let each rank for its own search ("trade scholarship Aurora," "how to refer a student"). Peer orgs that bury everything on one page lose that surface area.

03

Make the entity unmistakable

Consistent naming (JCD Foundation = John C. Dunham), Organization + FAQ schema, and explicit links to the Dunham Foundation teach search engines exactly who JCD is and how to describe us.

04

Evergreen body, fresh facts

AI engines favor recently-updated pages. Structure stays stable; dates, dollar figures, deadlines, and stories get refreshed each cycle so the site reads current the day a counselor checks it.

05

Differentiate, don't conform

The trades-scholarship space is a wall of navy-red-flag sameness. JCD's blue, civic-editorial, foundation-grade voice is the differentiator — the content should sound calm and credible, not rah-rah patriotic.

06

Proof over claims

Named schools, real student stories, and plain transparency on where money goes (institutions and employers, never individuals) build the trust funders and families need before they act.

Under the hood

The AEO layer.

Content wins the answer; structured data wins the citation. These ship alongside the pages so AI engines can read JCD without guessing.

Organization / NGO schema

Name, logo, address, founder (John C. Dunham), and a sameAs link to the Dunham Foundation — teaches engines who JCD is and how we relate to the parent.

FAQPage schema

On /faq and the mini-FAQs in Apply & the hubs, so individual answers are eligible to surface directly in AI Overviews and rich results.

BreadcrumbList + clean URLs

Readable, intent-matched paths (/students, /what-we-fund) and breadcrumbs that make the flat hierarchy explicit to crawlers.

Consistent entity naming

"JCD Foundation" and "John C. Dunham" used the same way site-wide, with a one-line definition repeated near the top of key pages for quotability.

Answer-first formatting

Short, declarative opening sentences; question-shaped H2s; scannable lists and steps — the formats answer engines extract most.

Freshness hygiene

Dated content (deadlines, awards, stats) reviewed each cycle. Sitemap + lastmod so engines see the site is current.

What peers do

Borrowed & avoided.

A read on comparable scholarship/foundation sites — what's worth taking, and where JCD's differentiation lane stays clear.

Org Does well JCD take
Jack Kent Cooke Foundation Crisp, standalone "How to Apply" with the process broken into discrete, dated steps. Borrow: the dated step structure on /apply.
Landscape Architecture Foundation Splits eligibility/guidelines from a deep, well-grouped Scholarship FAQ. Borrow: separate eligibility from a schema-marked FAQ.
Community foundations Unified application that auto-matches a student to every award they qualify for. Borrow: one application, matched in Airtable — not many forms.
mikeroweWORKS Work-ethic storytelling and a strong content/scholarship blend. Borrow: story-led hubs. Avoid: the red-black industrial styling.
Horatio Alger / SkillsUSA / ABC Scholar stories and recognizable, heavy branding. Avoid: navy-red-flag sameness — JCD stays blue, civic-editorial.
The throughline: peers win on clear eligibility, a single clean application, deep FAQs, and real stories — so JCD matches all four. Where they converge on patriotic, heavy, navy-red branding, JCD deliberately diverges. Same rigor, opposite tone.
