What changed since round 1

Three new constraints. Better marks.

Lesson 01

The mark must flex over time

Bob's noted that JCD's focus areas may evolve — Manufacturing / Health / Trades today, but possibly different tomorrow. So nothing in the mark itself encodes "three." No triple tiles, no sector colors baked in, no triptychs. The mark says JCD; the sectors live in the broader brand system, where they can be added or renumbered.

Lesson 02

Don't compete on industrial

Round 1 leaned heavily into stencil and steel-plate territory — but that's exactly where Mike Rowe Works, SkillsUSA, and ABC already live. To be findable in this space, JCD needs to lean foundation, not factory: civic, editorial, endowment-grade.

Lesson 03

Letter construction, not letter dressing

Every direction this round explores a genuinely different way to draw the letters — interlocking, modernist, letterpressed, risographed, contained, classical Roman. No decorative shapes wrapped around plain letters. The construction is the design.

First pass · the mark we're replacing

Bob's note · May 2026
"The logo we forwarded is our first pass. I think the wave action around the JCD overpowers the name of the organization. I would be very open to your ideas for using the letters JCD in another fashion like the orange letter logo you showed us previously in some shade or shades of blue."
— Bob

Bob's two asks land cleanly: kill the wave action, and let the letters themselves carry the brand in blue. Round 2 takes both seriously — and adds Bob's earlier note that the foundation's focus areas may shift over time, which means the mark can't encode any particular set of sectors today.

The pattern

Navy + red flags + heavy display type.

Five of six real competitors lean on the same playbook: flag iconography, patriotic navy-and-red palette, and heavy display typography. The one outlier (Dunham) is deliberately quiet and almost invisible. There's a wide-open lane for a scholarship foundation that's confident, modern, blue-only, and built on a memorable letterform — not a flag, not a swoosh, not "another navy + red trades org."

The three rules of round 2

Letters first. Sectors silent. Built to flex.

Three constraints guided every concept on this page. They come directly from Bob's notes and from what the competitive landscape opens up for JCD.

Rule 01

The letterform is the design

No swooshes, rings, orbits, halos, or chrome. Every mark is built from how J, C, and D are constructed — interlocked, modernist, letterpressed, risographed, contained, classical. The letters are the entire idea.

Rule 02

The mark says nothing about sectors

JCD's focus may grow, shift, or narrow over time. So no triptychs, no three-tile patterns, no Mfg-Health-Trades color trio baked into the logo. Sector identity lives in page badges and photography — where it can change freely.

Rule 03

Lean foundation, not factory

The trades-org space is crowded with navy + red and heavy industrial display type. JCD wins by leaning the other way: civic, editorial, endowment-grade. Blue only, with neutrals as support.

Six new contenders

The round 2 marks.

Each card includes a flexibility score (how well the mark survives focus-area changes) and a differentiation score (how far it sits from the navy-red-flag pattern above). Both are read as 4-bar scales — more filled bars is better.

01

Interlocking Monogram

three letters, one mark

The J, C, and D share strokes — they're physically fused into a single navy letterform that reads as one mark first, then resolves into JCD. Pure type, pure construction. The most ownable of the six because nobody else has this exact letterform.

Letterpress Classical

the Carnegie register

A refined classical serif JCD, hand-pressed in navy ink onto cotton paper. The opposite of the swoosh logo — quiet, dignified, endowment-coded. Lives most beautifully on certificates, award letters, and donor materials. The hand-craft texture gives it warmth the all-digital marks can't.

Risograph Print

made for the audience, not the donors

Heavy sans-serif JCD printed with two color layers slightly out of register — the unmistakable risograph zine look. Modern, editorial, with energy. Two more · closer to the first pass

Same DNA as the original. Calmer wave.

By request: two options that honor what's good in the first-pass logo — the bold italic letters, the sense of forward motion, the blue-on-blue family feel — but rebalanced so the JCD letters are unmistakably the hero and the wave element supports them instead of orbiting them. The same fix in both: one small dynamic element, never two; never larger than the letters; never wrapping around behind them.

07

Tamed Original

the first-pass spirit, rebalanced

The closest read to the original's silhouette. Heavy italic JCD letters in navy with a subtle steel-blue inner ridge (the dignified echo of the chrome dimension, without the shine). A single small blue arc sits underneath the letters as a supporting ribbon — same family as the original swoosh, but contained, single, and clearly subordinate to the name.

08

Forward Arc

motion without the orbit

The italic energy of the original, the wave reduced to a single decision. Solid navy JCD letters lean forward; a single thin sky-blue line beneath starts level under the J and rises past the D — a forward-and-upward gesture that reads as growth, momentum, trajectory. One stroke does the work of the original's two competing swooshes.
