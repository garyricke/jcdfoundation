Bringing John to life

The video Bob sent runs about twenty minutes and has some wonderful photographs in it — John as a young man, the Equipto storefront, him laughing in his later years, the painted portrait. I pulled those frames out as stills and then animated each one into a short moving clip. Nothing is invented: the camera moves, the light shifts, the grain drifts. John himself doesn't do anything he didn't do. It just stops being a slideshow and starts feeling like footage.

Five of them are cut together into a silent loop behind the page, in the order of a life: the young man, the company, the portrait, the laugh, the painting. The Equipto shot is my favourite — the camera drifts down off the sign onto the handshake. It's the only image we have that shows the business, so it carries a lot.

We can add more of these. Each clip is quick to make, so if either of you has other photographs — Equipto floor, plant, people, anything from the family — send them over and I'll fold them into the loop. More material makes it better.

The narration

The twenty-minute video is full of good history, but it isn't built for a seventeen-year-old deciding what to do after graduation. So I used it as the source and wrote something new from it — the same story, pointed at the person we want to reach, and cut down to three minutes.

It opens on the thing that kid has probably already been told: that the trades are what you do if college doesn't work out. Then it spends two minutes showing why that's backwards, using John — he swept the floor of his father's factory, he graduated from West Aurora in 1928, his father made him sell on commission at twenty-one with a newborn at home. It closes on what JCD actually offers and the question the application asks.

A few things from the video I'd never heard before and put straight in:

  • John graduated from West Aurora High School — one of the twelve schools we fund. That may be the single best fact we have.
  • His “rules to live by.” His own list. Always do the worst job first. Don't look backwards and rehash things. It's on the page as its own section, and it lines up almost exactly with what Bob said we ask of students.
  • He knew everyone in the plant by first name, right through to being chairman.

On the page you press play, the headline steps aside, and the audio plays over a waveform with the words appearing as they're spoken. You can click anywhere on the waveform to jump around. The pictures keep running behind it the whole time.
