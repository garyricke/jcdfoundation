Fairview Dental · Aurora · Aug 20 2026

Nobody was
funding this path.

We spent an hour at one of the largest dental practices in Kane County and asked five people the same thing: what does this career actually pay, what does it cost to get into, and who helps you get there. The last answer was the one we didn’t expect.

Here’s what they told us. ↓

Why we went

The program hasn’t started. The path already exists.

JCD hasn’t funded a student yet — the first grant window opens in January 2027. So we can’t show you results. What we can show you is whether the road we intend to pay for actually leads anywhere. Fairview Dental employs sixteen hygienists across sixteen chairs in Aurora. Four dentists and two hygienists sat down with us on a Thursday lunch hour. Nobody was paid, nobody was coached, and nothing below is a JCD claim — it’s theirs.

Who we talked to

Five people, one practice.

Four of them own it. Two of them are the career we’re trying to send students into. Tap any of them to hear the piece we’d put on the public page.

The numbers

What it pays. What it costs. How long it takes.

Every figure below came out of someone’s mouth on camera. The pay figure was given independently by three different owners who were not in the room together.

$45/hr
Starting pay for a new hygiene graduate
Stated separately by Dr. Cardenas, Dr. Gorenz and Dr. Dominguez. Plus 401(k), PTO, medical, holiday pay and free dental work.
$20–30K
What the two hygienists paid to qualify
Cassie: $20–25K at Illinois Valley CC. Bethany: $25–30K at College of DuPage. This is the gap JCD closes.
3–4 yrs
From high school to working, prerequisites included
The degree itself is two years. Cassie took 3.5 years door to door; Bethany took 4. No four-year university required.
What it costs to qualify $20,000–30,000
First-year earnings at $45/hr ~$93,600

How we got that second number: the $45/hour is theirs. The annual figure is our arithmetic — $45 × 40 hours × 52 weeks, before tax and before any of the benefits they described. Nobody at Fairview stated an annual salary, and part-time schedules are common in the field. We show it because the shape of the comparison is the point: one year of the job covers the whole cost of getting into it, three times over.

The finding

The money that exists goes somewhere else.

We asked both hygienists whether there were scholarships for what they did. We expected a yes. We got the clearest argument for this foundation that anyone has made to us yet — and they made it twice, about two different colleges.

“We were eliminated from three quarters of the scholarships right away, because they were directly for the nursing program.”
Cassie · Illinois Valley Community College

“COD has barely any dental hygiene scholarship specific. And they do have the STEM field scholarships, but when I tried to apply, some of them were like — your degree isn’t part of that STEM field.”

Bethany · College of DuPage

Nursing takes the healthcare money. STEM rules them out for being healthcare. A career with a documented shortage, a $45 starting wage and a two-year credential has almost nothing pointed at it. That is the hole JCD was built to fill — and until this interview, we were only guessing it was there.

Bethany, on being ruled out of STEM

In their words

Everything we asked.

Read it or watch it — every answer below is written out as well as filmed. Expand a question to get the clip and the quote together.

What the employers say
How badly do you actually need hygienists?
It’s a well-known fact that there’s a shortage of hygienists out there… With that shortage has come higher pay. I think hygienists are making more than they ever have at this time. So it’s a great time to get into it. Dr. David Gorenz
Dr. Dominguez, separately: “It is hard to have a fully staffed hygiene team… a lot of those dentists are doing a lot of the cleanings themselves. I do see a big need for hygiene. It’s a field that I never see is gonna go away.”
Is anyone already building a pipeline into this job?
There’s currently no partnership with any kind of organization right now where we can mingle with potential prospective hygienists. So I think that’s lacking in the marketplace. Dr. Salvador Cardenas
Asked what a JCD-sponsored student might do, Dr. Dominguez said: “I have not come across an intern opportunity or seen that in the dental field, but that sounds like an incredible idea… I could see that program thriving.”
Does class rank matter when you hire?
High energy… a drive to want to help people. Patients come first. An ability to put others before yourself. Interpersonal skills is really big. Dr. David Gorenz
Not one of the four owners named grades, class rank or a GPA when asked what makes a good hire. Dr. Dominguez: “someone that has a strong character — able to talk to people, carry on a conversation, hard worker, a team player.”
Would you host a student who wanted to see the job first?
West Aurora High School has a program… every semester they’re gonna send us one senior to shadow us, for credit for them. They’d rotate — hang out with the hygienist for a week or two, then the assistant, then the back with the insurance gals. Dr. Salvador Cardenas
He also went to West Aurora — the same high school John C. Dunham graduated from in 1928. On the students’ reaction: “They were stoked. When we were in high school we didn’t have stuff like that.”
How much does the practice depend on hygiene?
Hygiene is the engine that keeps our practice going. We have a hundred patients coming in for hygiene every day. That is what most of our patients are coming for. Dr. David Gorenz
Dr. Dominguez put it differently: “Hygiene is the heart and soul of our practice.” Fairview runs 16 operatories and books more than 20,000 cleanings and exams a year across roughly 50 employees.
What the hygienists say
Did you have to be a straight-A student?
I won’t lie, I was the straight-A student — but I had a lot of classmates that had to retake anatomy, that were B’s, C’s, but still got into the program. That goes to show that you don’t have to be straight-A to get in. Bethany · College of DuPage
Both programs admitted on a blind point sheet — “each student was a number, they had no idea who the student was.” Grades count, but so does prior dental experience.
Did you feel pressure to do a four-year degree instead?
Part of the draw is the fact that it is a shorter-term program — that you don’t have to go to a four-year university to achieve it. Cassie · Illinois Valley Community College
Worth being precise about: the hygiene degree itself is two years, and Dr. Gorenz confirmed a bachelor’s adds nothing but “the prestige — from our standpoint, nothing really.” But both hygienists needed prerequisites first. Door to door it took Cassie 3.5 years and Bethany 4.
Is it hard to get a seat?
My program in particular only accepts eight students per cohort… I know of other hygienists that waited two, maybe three years on a wait list before they even got into their program. Cassie · Illinois Valley Community College
This changes what JCD is for. College of DuPage takes about 30 from a pool of 100-plus, with in-district preference. Money isn’t the only barrier here — seats are. A grant that arrives without a seat attached solves half the problem.
What kind of person is this job for?
I’m more of an introverted kind of person, and it’s more of an extroverted job than I realized. But it’s made me come out of my shell… I was someone that only would talk to people that I knew. Bethany · College of DuPage
Cassie, who calls herself the opposite: “someone that’s got a good work ethic and is willing to put in the work and has determination to be somewhere does great in a career like this.” Neither of them described a type.
Where does it go after the first job?
Whether you stop after two years of a junior college or you decide to go on — it’s amazing. Dr. Michele Bruno
She started as a hygienist at Parkland, worked a couple of years, then went to UIC dental school and graduated in 1993 — ending up an owner of both the practice and the building. At Fairview, assistants regularly go back to school, become hygienists and return. The two-year credential is a door, not a ceiling.

So what does this change?

Three things. The grant size now has a real number behind it — $20–30K is what these two actually paid. The “we don’t care about your class rank” promise is no longer just our position; it’s what four employers said unprompted. And we learned that the scholarship money already out there is pointed somewhere else entirely.

One thing we got wrong going in: we assumed money was the barrier. For dental hygiene, a seat is just as scarce. That’s a partnership problem, and Fairview has already offered to help solve it.
