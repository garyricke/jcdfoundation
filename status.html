Milestone Cleared

501(c)(3) approved — the gate is open

Bob, Friday afternoon: “Just got the formal notification from the IRS that JCD Foundation has been approved for our tax-exempt status as a 501(c)(3) private operating foundation. We now have a clear runway for moving forward with our marketing plan.” Filed April 1, approved in under four months — ahead of Bob's own June 26 estimate of “four months out at the latest.”

What this unblocks immediately: the one structural condition on the whole engagement — no marketing spend until the determination letter lands — is satisfied. Phase 2 activation ($2K–$5K) can fire. The password gate can come off and the public site can go live. School, teacher and employer outreach can run without the “pre-501(c)(3)” caveat on every page.

The status matters, not just the approval. A private operating foundation is the specific structure Bob was counting on in the June 26 meeting when he said: “Because we're an operating foundation, we can reimburse the people for those costs” — transportation, tools, boots, work clothing paid to the student rather than routed through a college. That's the leading answer to the open disbursement question. Worth one call to JCD's counsel or accountant before it's promised in public copy: private foundations making grants directly to individuals for study or training generally need IRS advance approval of their grant-selection procedures (IRC §4945(g)). Confirm whether that was included in the application or is a separate filing — it affects whether the Jan 1 window can pay students directly.

Build Proof of concept

Hero rebuilt as a listening experience — waveform + live subtitles

Per Gary's direction, the small “Listen to the story” pill is gone. In its place: a 132px play button sitting to the right of the headline. Press it and the headline and description fade out, a full-width waveform fades in, and the narration's subtitles animate over it line by line. The archival montage keeps looping behind the whole thing.

The waveform is real, not decorative. 320 buckets of RMS measured off the actual MP3 with ffmpeg, so the shape genuinely matches the audio — played bars in brass, unplayed in dim white, with a playhead. On top of that it moves live: Cloudinary serves access-control-allow-origin: *, so a crossorigin audio element can be fed through a Web Audio AnalyserNode. Bars within ten buckets of the playhead swell with the signal, which reads as a pulse travelling along the track as it plays. If the AudioContext is ever unavailable the bars still render and still track position — they just stop pulsing.

Subtitles took two passes. First cut used Whisper's own 42-character segments and broke mid-phrase — “trades are what you do if college doesn't”. Rebuilt by aligning Whisper's word timings against the canonical script with difflib, then re-chunking on clause boundaries instead of character count. 59 cues now, whole sentences, and the text is the script's rather than the transcriber's — which also fixes Whisper mishearing “the man this foundation” as “a man.”

Verified: sync holds at 0:09, 1:37 and through the final line; the montage keeps looping throughout; ended, Close and Escape all return cleanly to the idle hero with the audio reset; and at 390px the button stacks under the copy with no horizontal overflow. Cues and peaks are inlined (about 5.5 KB total) so there's no extra request and nothing to CORS-fail. prefers-reduced-motion skips the analyser entirely.

Scrubbing added (same day). The waveform is now a real transport: click anywhere to jump, drag to scrub, and a small time label tracks the cursor so you can see where you'd land before committing. It's also keyboard operable — focus it and use ←/→ for ±5s (shift for 15s), Home/End, space to play-pause — and exposed as role="slider" with a live aria-valuetext, so it's usable without a mouse or a screen.

Subtitles re-cut to single lines. Gary preferred them not wrapping, so the cue table was rebuilt with a 46-character cap, breaking on clause boundaries where one exists and word boundaries otherwise: 79 cues, averaging 2.3s each, which is normal subtitle pacing. A fitSub() pass shrinks the type if a line would still overflow at an odd viewport width.

One deliberate exception on phones. Holding one line at 390px would have forced the longest cue down to 13px — measured, not guessed. That's too small to read, so below 560px the subtitle is allowed to wrap to two lines at 21px instead, and fitSub() stands down when it sees wrapping is permitted. One line everywhere from 560px up (30px at desktop, 22px at tablet); two readable lines below that. Verified across 1440 / 900 / 700 / 560 / 390 with no horizontal overflow at any width.

Still outstanding: more clips in the montage, per Gary's note.

Build Live

Hero voiceover — script written, audio delivered

Script at plan/vo-script-about-hero.md, ready to paste into ElevenLabs. Version 3 — v1 was a tidy historical sketch that pivoted to policy and the seam showed; v2 rebuilt it around the kid actually listening; v3 adds a closer that lands on John's own vision. 518 words, ~3:40, with a marked trim guide down to ~2:55 if it wants to be shorter.

The closer was Gary's note, and it fixed a real weakness. The script used to end on “We're going to ask what you want to do” — which describes the question instead of asking it. It now carries on into why that's the question: John spent the end of his life pushing people to go after what they actually wanted, and someone who knew him said “John had a wonderful life, because John chose to have a wonderful life.” Then it hands the choice over — “That's the part nobody can do for you. The rest — tuition, tools, boots, the ride there — that part we can do.” — and ends by asking the listener directly: “So. What do you want to do?” That's the literal application question, now spoken to them rather than about them.

It opens on the listener, not on 1928: “Somebody has probably told you that the trades are what you do if college doesn't work out. That's backwards — and the man this foundation is named for would have told you so himself.” Everything after that is evidence for the claim.

The spine is a coincidence that isn't one. The documentary records that when John backed students he wanted “get-up-and-go and drive… somebody who'd already proven something to themselves” — not grades. Eighty years later, asked to picture the student JCD most wants to reach, Bob said “self-motivated, hard worker, has their sights on what they want to do.” Same person, same standard, two lifetimes apart, arrived at independently. That's the argument the whole script hangs on.

Other beats now doing real work: the 1931 commission line from John's father is reframed as a shot with conditions on it, which sets up JCD's own end of the bargain — help, but you show up. Bob's $200 boots land as the concrete proof that tuition isn't what stops people. And it closes on the literal application question: “We're not going to ask you what you've overcome. We're going to ask what you want to do.”

Every line is sourced, and the file carries the table: seven claims come from the newly transcribed documentary with timestamps, the rest from the June 26 meeting. Deliberately excluded — the Dunham Fund (never named), Bob (the page speaks institutionally), the Texas/Pennsylvania expansion and the “global leader” line (both still [TK]), any impact claim, and the “wife was a nurse” line. That last one matters most: it's contradicted by the documentary and unresolved, and it must not get baked into recorded audio before Bob settles it. Re-recording costs money; leaving it out costs nothing.

The play button is already in the hero and already works. Brass pill under the hand-written line: circular play/pause icon, label, a live countdown, and a thin progress fill along the bottom edge. Keyboard-focusable with aria-pressed tracking state. It reads its own duration from the file's metadata rather than a hard-coded number.

Score prompt written too — plan/suno-prompt-about-vo-score.md. Primary direction is a sparse heartland acoustic bed (fingerpicked guitar, upright bass, brushed drums, pedal steel, 74 BPM, tape saturation), plus two alternates: a warm-industrial version that leans into the Equipto footage, and a minimal felt-piano version that's the safest under a voice. The file names the failure mode explicitly, because Suno defaults straight into it: inspirational nonprofit score — rising piano, strings at forty seconds, major-key swell under the call to action. That would undo the tone of the whole site. The brief is a bed, not a piece of music; if you notice it, it's wrong. Includes how to judge takes, the practical workflow (Suno won't hit narration beats on request — generate a flat bed and cut it to the read), mixing numbers, and a reminder to check the Suno tier's commercial-use terms before this sits on a public 501(c)(3) site.

Audio delivered and live — 3:02. Gary supplied the ElevenLabs read (2:59) and a Suno bed (3:09). Mixed, mastered and on Cloudinary at jcd/jcd-dunham-vo; the hero button plays from the CDN.

The mix. The bed arrived louder than the narration (−14.9 vs −18.1 LUFS), so it needed real work rather than a fader move. What it got: a 4 dB dip at 2 kHz to clear the speech band, −9 dB static trim, and sidechain ducking keyed off the voice (6:1, 15 ms attack, 450 ms release) so the music breathes back up in the script's pauses instead of sitting flat underneath. Final master −15.7 LUFS, −1.8 dBTP.

Two structural choices. The voice enters at 3.5 seconds, so the bed establishes before anyone speaks. And the music fades out entirely by 2:56 — the last four lines play dry, so “So. What do you want to do?” lands with nothing behind it. Verified by silence detection: zero silent gaps under the body (the bed fills every pause), true silence under the closer.

Served untransformed, deliberately. Cloudinary's q_auto re-encodes this file ~7% larger (3.14 MB vs the 2.93 MB source) because it's already a 128 kbps encode. q_auto:eco saves 80 KB at the cost of a second encode pass. Neither is worth it — the raw delivery URL is the right call here, which is the opposite of the video, where q_auto saved 800 KB. Worth remembering: audio and video don't behave the same way through Cloudinary.

The claim has been left on the page as Bob stated it, with an internal flag beside it — he may simply know something the video doesn't say. Also in there, unused so far: born Nutley NJ, Sept 9 1910, in Aurora from age three; North Central and Babson Colleges; Old Second National Bank board for 30 years; real tragedy — his son Jack died in 1979 at 48 and his granddaughter Jean Marie in 1986 at 18; a salesman's creed of under-promise and over-deliver; and a striking note that he thought selling was everyone's job, the president's most of all. May 28, 2026 · Working session · Bob, Gary & Jack

What we covered.

A working pass over the outreach materials and the summer field plan. The headline decisions: a single, ego-free business-card system; a sharper read on what JCD actually funds; and a mapped interview sprint to fill the site with real voices while school is still in session.

01 Business card system

  • Org over individual. The card leads with JCD Foundation; the person's name is secondary. Bob likes the Butcher & Wolf (Batavia insurance) layout and the navy + gold treatment we used.
  • Names, no titles. One standard format, three cards — Bob, Jack, and Jen. No titles on anyone: nobody outranks anyone, no underlings.
  • Who carries which card. A 76-year-old isn't knocking on school doors. Jack and Jen run the in-person school / teacher / student visits; Bob works behind the scenes and handles the senior relationships (Waubonsee, Rush Copley). Gary offered to help on school visits too — Bob is open to it.
  • Phone number. Bob will add a number that routes through a recorder to a message service — not for taking calls from kids. Applicants always go online; no callbacks. The line is for other adults to leave a message. Number (supplied by Bob, June 1): 630-454-4222 — goes active ~June 4.
  • Domain fix. Correct the web address on the card to the registered foundation domain — the plain name had already been harvested, so the registered domain is thejcdfoundation.org — the "the" is required because the plain jcdfoundation.org had already been taken.
  • Slogan rethink. Bob is nervous about "Earn As You Learn" on the flip side — it's true for the trades (work by day, class by night) but not for nursing or academic paths that start school-first. He'd rather under-promise and over-deliver. Find a line that holds across every funded path.
  • Mark idea. Bob likes the idea of a small swoosh-style mark at the base of the logo (Nike / Amazon feel). Worth exploring.

02 What JCD actually funds

  • Bob clarified the deposit categories so the copy is accurate: tuition, tuition and fees, and apprenticeship startup costs — chiefly tools, which run steep in the technical trades (electrician, HVAC).
  • Healthcare / nursing is a funded path too, but it's academics-first — which is exactly why "earn as you learn" can't be a blanket promise.

03 Summer interview & outreach sprint

  • Timing is good. Community colleges are in (or near) summer term — a more relaxed window to get in and interview.
  • Building trades. Bob meets the head of the building trades next week (electrical / HVAC, non-union) — a likely interview. Goal: surface the real barriers. The big one he expects — you can't enter an apprenticeship without an employer willing to take an apprentice. He cites a 25-year-old already running older crews as proof young people can advance fast.
  • Waubonsee. The new tech center / welding program (among the best in Illinois). The college president is a Batavia resident and proud of the build — a warm door.
  • High schools. East Aurora, West Aurora, and Kaneland all run programs that send kids into jobs straight out of high school, with employers later paying for more training.
  • Community colleges in the mix: Waubonsee, College of DuPage, and (less so) Elgin.
  • Advanced manufacturing / machining. Bob wants into the plants — tie in the Valley Industrial Association's recruiting push and shops like Jake's Machine in Aurora. Lots of retirements opening voids in EDM and mold making.
  • Rush Copley. The CEO (Bob's former neighbor) will point to the right spokesperson rather than speak himself. The aim: someone who can speak to the demand and the future for kids in these fields. Possible tie-in with Gary's Live Healthy Chicago work at Rush.
  • Future asset. A year out, interview funded students for progress reports — recruiting fuel for the next cohort.

04 Tools & workflow

  • Interview capture. Bob is ordering an Amazon voice-recorder rig (plugs into a phone, works iOS / Android) to record interviews in the field and send to Gary to ingest into the plan. He'll send the link.
  • Email engine. E-blasts run through EmailOctopus (free tier ~2,000) — AI drafts the email, drop in the list, track opens, list validation built in. Jack owns it; Gary will train him.
  • Short video over long copy. Nobody reads a three-page email — a 30-second to two-minute video gets watched. JCD content leans on short, character-driven interview video.
  • Printing. Bob keeps his own long-standing printer (loyalty). We just deliver print-ready PDFs from the page; Gary to wire the print-to-PDF / print-ready export.

05 The "why now" case

  • AI is hollowing out entry-level white-collar work — there's no better moment to push kids toward the trades.
  • COVID already proved the point: the essential workers were the trades.
  • The cultural headwind to counter — in many high schools, kids who choose the trades are treated as "not smart." JCD exists to flip that.
